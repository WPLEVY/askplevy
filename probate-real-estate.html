Probate Real Estate in California

By William Plevy

Real estate in probate can involve court authority, fiduciary duties, title requirements, sale procedures, beneficiary interests, and practical property management. Families should not assume property can be sold or transferred informally.

Authority to Act

The personal representative generally needs legal authority before selling or transferring probate property. Depending on the estate, this may involve letters testamentary, letters of administration, court orders, or authority under the Independent Administration of Estates Act.

Title companies, buyers, brokers, and escrow officers will usually need evidence that the correct person has authority to sign.

Managing the Property During Probate

While probate is pending, someone must protect the property. That may include insurance, utilities, repairs, security, mortgage payments, taxes, and decisions about occupancy.

Vacant properties can deteriorate quickly. Occupied properties can create different issues.

Selling Probate Real Estate

A probate sale may require specific procedures depending on the level of authority granted and the facts of the estate.

The personal representative should consider pricing, disclosures, repairs, broker selection, offers, court timing, and beneficiary communications.
